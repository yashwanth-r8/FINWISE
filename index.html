Survey Results

Financial Challenges & Decision-Making Survey. Anonymous survey of working adults aged 25 to 45 (n = 8).

62.5%of respondents are aged 25-35
37.5%find expense management difficult
50%name Loans / EMIs as a top challenge
37.5%say too many options hurt planning

Key findings

  • Debt servicing dominates. Loans / EMIs appear in the top challenges, hard-to-manage areas and wish-list answers across both age groups.
  • The saving gap. Saving is both a top challenge and a top confusion area, closely linked to unexpected expenses.
  • Clarity beats information. Respondents are overwhelmed by choice and want trustworthy, simple direction.
  • Long-term planning is out of focus. Retirement planning and taxes received 0%; immediate cash-flow pressure takes priority.
  • Income allocation is a hidden pain. Splitting a monthly salary across many needs surfaced as an explicit problem.

How hard is managing monthly expenses?

No respondent finds expense management easy. 37.5% find it somewhat or very difficult, and most sit in the uncomfortable middle.

Biggest financial challenge

Half of respondents name Loans / EMIs. Retirement planning and taxes were not selected by anyone, so present-day cash flow outweighs long-term planning.

Hardest areas to manage or understand

Saving and Investing are tied as the most confusing areas. The problem is the lack of a clear, simple method, not only a shortage of money.

How respondents track expenses

Nobody uses a spreadsheet and 12.5% do not track at all. Tracking alone has not translated into a working budget.

What makes planning difficult

Too many financial options is the leading barrier. Decision fatigue, not lack of knowledge, is the core issue.

Biggest challenge by age group

25-35 years (n = 5)36-45 years (n = 3)
Ages 25-35 are digital in their habits but unsure how to grow and protect money. Ages 36-45 carry heavier obligations such as children's education and medical costs. Loans / EMIs cut across both groups. Groups are small, so read these as indicative.

Methodology and limitations

Anonymous survey with closed-ended and open-ended questions. Sample size is n = 8 (25-35 years: 5; 36-45 years: 3), so findings are indicative rather than statistically conclusive and highlight early patterns to guide larger data collection. Where respondents chose multiple options, all selections were counted, so totals may exceed 100%.
